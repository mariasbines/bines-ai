This week Anthropic shipped Fable 5, their new frontier model. It sits in a tier above everything else they sell, including whatever was the best model last month. I build with these things for a living, and the tooling I use every day switched over to it more or less immediately. It costs double its predecessor on the API. And the detail I can't stop thinking about is what happens on the 23rd of June: until the 22nd, Fable is included in the ordinary paid plans. Then it comes off them. After that, the frontier is metered — you buy usage credits, and you pay per thought.

Anthropic says this is temporary, a capacity thing, and that they intend to put it back in the plans as soon as they can. I believe them. It doesn't change what just got demonstrated: when intelligence is scarce, it goes on a meter, and the meter starts at the top.

Meanwhile, the assistant in my kitchen came free with the Prime subscription.

So I now live at both ends of an interesting spectrum. By day I work with the most expensive intelligence money can rent. By night I get horoscopes from the free kind — postcard #016 has the haiku it wrote me about my day, which fit every day anyone has ever had. The obvious lesson is *you get what you pay for*. Which is true, and is also the least interesting thing you can say about it.

Because "you get what you pay for" assumes you can see what you paid for. With a washing machine you can. The cheap one leaves your clothes damp; you notice; you save up for the better one. Intelligence doesn't work like that. The cheap tier isn't visibly worse, it's invisibly worse. It answers fluently, confidently, warmly. It writes you a haiku.

<PullQuote>The one skill the free tier has genuinely mastered is feeling like enough.</PullQuote>

We've had unequal access to intelligence forever — that's what a lawyer's hourly rate is. The new part is the cheap tier doing an impression of the expensive one, well enough that you stop asking what you're missing. A bad lawyer loses your case and you find out. A free model gives you a worse answer than the frontier would have, and there's no verdict. No damp clothes. Nothing. You walk away satisfied, which is precisely what it's optimised for.

I notice the gap because I commute across it daily. Most people won't get to. If you've only ever used what came free with the shopping, the frontier isn't something you're missing — it's something you've never had a reason to believe exists.

Fable goes on the meter on the 23rd of June. The haiku, of course, stays free.
